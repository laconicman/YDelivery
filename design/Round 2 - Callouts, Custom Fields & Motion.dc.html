Round 2 · callouts, custom fields, motion

The map as a second surface, and the order as your record

Three answers. Callouts give the fixed-card shell somewhere to put per-stop detail that would otherwise force every row to three lines. Custom fields make the order findable by the identifiers a business actually uses — defined once for the organization, carried by every order. The motion spec keeps animation tied to state changes rather than decoration.

4a map callouts · 4b custom fields · 4c motion & deployment target.

4a

Callouts carry what the row can't

The division of labour: the row answers "is this the right place", the callout answers "what happens when the courier gets there". Entrance, floor, flat, door code, who meets them, what they hand over, and — once the order is live — when they arrived. None of that belongs in a list row, and all of it is what makes a delivery succeed. Tapping a pin opens the callout; tapping the callout opens the full point editor. The row stays two lines forever.

One rule: a callout is never the only route to anything. It is a shortcut for people who think in maps, and a VoiceOver user reaches the same content through the row's disclosure.

4b

Your fields, defined once

The insight from your note: which identifiers matter differs between organizations and is identical inside one. So the schema is a setting, not a per-order chore. Define «Заказ», «Счёт», «Платёж» once; every new delivery shows those rows, pre-typed and validated; history is searchable by their values. Three of them map onto fields the API already carries (extraId, externalOrderId, shippingDocument) and travel with the order — the rest stay local, which is worth saying in the UI once, quietly.

Two flags per field, and its own section. isOptional decides whether the order can leave without it — a required field blocks ordering and says so in place, beside the field, never in an alert. isShownByDefault decides whether it sits in the section from the start or waits behind «Добавить поле» — which is what stops a ten-field schema becoming a ten-row wall for the eight orders in ten that need three of them. Required implies shown: the pair cannot express «обязательное, но скрытое», and the editor should say so rather than allow it. Ваши поля is a section of its own, not rows mixed into the order form.

4c

Motion — one rule, then a table

The rule: animation reports a state change the user did not cause, or confirms one they did — never decorates a static screen. A parcel app is used one-handed, in a hurry, often outdoors. Symbol effects are nearly free and they earn their place because a moving glyph is legible at a glance; a spring on a card is not.

MomentEffectWhy it earns itReduce Motion
Ищем курьера .symbolEffect(.variableColor) The one genuinely indeterminate wait; the glyph is the spinner static + text
Статус сменился .contentTransition(.symbolEffect(.replace)) Arrives while you're looking elsewhere — the swap is the notification cross-fade
Цены пришли .numericText() + shimmer→value Price landing in a strip you were already reading must not just blink instant
Точка подтверждена .symbolEffect(.bounce, value:) Confirms the tap landed when the map barely changes haptic only
Заказ создан .bounce + success haptic The one irreversible action in the app. No confetti — money just moved haptic only
Курьер двигается withAnimation(.linear) on coordinate Interpolate between polls; a teleporting scooter reads as a bug jump
Ошибка поля Pow .shake or 2-frame offset Only where the error is next to the input; never for network failures color + text
On Pow

Good library, and I'd take exactly two things from it: .shake for invalid input and a change effect on the price when it updates. The rest of its catalogue — spray, confetti, glare, jump — is built for consumer apps celebrating things. Here the celebration is that a stranger will carry your goods somewhere, which nobody wants glitter about.

Since it's two effects, both reproducible in ~20 lines, my recommendation is to skip the dependency and keep the animation vocabulary in the app, where the Reduce Motion branch is visible in the same file. Adopt Pow only if you want its transition set broadly.

On the deployment floor

Everything in the table above exists at iOS 17 — variable-colour, bounce, replace, numeric text. The floor buys you nothing to raise for motion alone.

iOS 18 would add the newer symbol effects (breathe, rotate, wiggle) and MapKit's own place-detail presentation. Of those, only breathe is tempting — it's the honest "waiting, nothing is wrong" idiom for «Ищем курьера».

My advice: hold at 17 until something structural wants a newer floor — the demo app already sits at 18, so the split is deliberate and documented. Raising the floor for one symbol effect trades real users for a nicer wait. Verify each effect and symbol in the SF Symbols app before it lands; the names above are from memory and the catalogue moves.
