Explaining tariffs, and hiding a large form

Three shapes for the beginner explainer, so you can judge them against each other rather than in the abstract. Then the part the dossier under-counts: your demo shows an order carries far more than two addresses — address parts, contacts with extension codes, per-item cost and currency and dimensions and point mapping, loaders bounded by tariff, a due date bounded by a window. The proposal is progressive disclosure with typed, self-formatting rows, not a Borzo wall of fields.

Options: 3a vertical list · 3b paged cards · 3c expand-in-strip · 3d parcel & options · 3e saved place · 3f accessibility-XL.

Parcel and options — the dense part, disclosed

The rule: one summary line per group, tappable, expanding to typed rows. Every constrained field states its constraint in place of a hint — loaders 0–2 and only on грузовой, due date inside the provider's window, currency a picker not a text field, dimensions in centimetres in the UI and metres on the wire. Nothing here uses API vocabulary.

Saved place = a whole point, not a coordinate

Your note about predefined data changes what a saved place is: address parts, a default contact, a default point type, even default options. Picking «Склад на Невском» then fills a route row completely, and the repeat-last-order path becomes one tap from history. This is the strongest argument for the fixed-card shell — filled rows have two lines, and they need to be visible without gestures.

3f

accessibility-extra-large — the reflow ladder

A ranked ladder, not breakpoints. In order: (1) label and value stack; (2) leading glyph keeps its size but the row grows; (3) the tariff strip becomes a vertical list — the horizontal strip cannot survive, which is why 3a is the explainer to build; (4) toolbar Cancel/Done wrap to their own row; (5) two-line address rows are allowed to run to three lines rather than truncate. Truncating an address is never acceptable — a wrong address is a failed delivery.
